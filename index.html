Method

How I approach enablement and adoption

Adults adopt AI when a real task gets easier, support is close at hand, and progress is measured after the workshop ends.

01

Map the work

I sit with a team, map the current workflow, and define the problem before anyone picks a tool. The habit comes from department work at Beaufort County School District, and it transfers directly to workforce enablement.

02

Build a proof of concept, then hand it off

I build a working proof of concept and train the host team to sustain it. Capability should not depend on me remaining in the room.

03

Teach in the role

I write curricula and playbooks, record a role-based video series, and deliver workshops, role-based training, and office hours on tools, risks, and responsible use.

04

Build a network, lead by influence

I built and run an AI champion and ambassador network and a center-of-excellence structure. From 2014 to 2023 I led a multi-school STEM and applied robotics program with machine learning as STEM Program Director at Ashland City Schools.

05

Measure more than attendance

Adoption, in my work, means repeated weekly use of useful workflows, quality of output after human review, support needs, and behavior that lasts past the session. Attendance is an input, not the outcome. The measurement design lives in the 90-day plan below.

06

Govern with care

As the district’s representative in the South Carolina Department of Education AI Pilot, I work with the Department, the Southern Regional Education Board, and a monthly university network on adoption methodology in a regulated environment. I evaluate tools for fit, risk, and responsible use.
