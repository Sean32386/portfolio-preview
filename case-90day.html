Sample 90-Day AI Adoption Plan

A 90-day rollout I would bring to a new organization and adapt to its tools, policy, and time standards. Figures tagged Target are planning models.

Status. Planning sample Cohort. About 300 trained staff, target Primary outcome. Weekly active use, not attendance

Problem

A workshop can fill a room and still leave weekly use unchanged. This plan treats attendance as an input. The outcome is whether trained staff use an approved tool on real work at least weekly, whether that work is reviewed, and whether the behavior lasts through 90 days.

The sample uses a school-district cohort because that is the setting I know. The same structure (baseline, role-based training, champions, office hours, a department workflow, and 30/60/90 measurement) transfers to a company, with that organization’s tools, policy, and time standards.

My responsibility

  • A baseline of current weekly use, taken before or at the first session and labeled as a survey result.
  • Role-based training, office hours, and short follow-up, rather than a single event.
  • A champion group with a defined selection rule, responsibilities, and an escalation path.
  • One department workflow, timed, with human review built into the after condition. In a school setting, a first candidate is staff policy and procedure lookup.
  • A simple dashboard of weekly use, hours, reach, and champion coverage, with every planning figure tagged as a target or estimate.

What I delivered

The 90-day structure I would stand up and adapt:

  • Champion model. The network structure already exists in my current work. Coverage target: 24 champions by day 90 (8% of 300), stretch 30 (10%). Selection favors practitioners who already use an approved tool and can coach peers, with coverage across sites and functions. Champions model one useful workflow, support colleagues, log barriers, and escalate policy or data questions. Support comes from playbooks, the role-based video series, and office hours. Short check-ins land at 30, 60, and 90 days. Privacy, student-data, or tool-approval issues go to the appropriate district or IT channel.
  • Department workflow. A first timed workflow in this setting is staff policy and procedure lookup: finding the right policy or procedure in existing documents, with citations and required human review.
  • Operating practices. A short list of approved tools, with a clear path to request something new. Data-handling rules that keep personal and regulated information out of unapproved systems. Human review before AI output is used in a decision or an external document. A simple quality check (source, accuracy, tone) built into the workflow. A named escalation path for privacy, legal, or technical issues.

Methodology. Net hours = 8.0 estimated baseline − 5.0 after time. Human review is already included in the after time and is subtracted once; it is not applied again as a discount on net hours or on dollar value. Annualized value at target adoption = 165 weekly users × 3.0 net hours × 37.4 instructional weeks × $82.13 = $1,520,473 (about $1.5M). First-year value assumes a linear ramp from the 7.5% estimated baseline (22.5 weekly users) to the 55% day-90 target (165 weekly users) over the first 13 instructional weeks, then 165 weekly users held for the remaining 24.4 weeks: 5,244.75 user-weeks × 3.0 × $82.13 = $1,292,254 (about $1.3M). The loaded hourly rate is an estimate based on a median BCSD 2026-27 MA step, 190 days, an 8-hour day, and a BLS June 2026 teacher benefits load. This model values staff time. It is not cash recovered and is not net ROI; implementation, license, training, and support costs sit outside it.

Quality and satisfaction

What will be measured

1
Session pulse. After each workshop, a short anonymous form on usefulness, clarity, and whether the person tried a real task during the session.
2
Confidence and quality. Baseline and follow-up items on confidence using an approved tool, plus whether output is reviewed before it is used. Quality means checked work, not speed alone.
3
Support needs. Office-hour topics, champion flags, and barriers (time, access, policy, trust) collected at 30, 60, and 90 days so the plan can change.
4
Sustained use. The primary outcome remains weekly active use in the trained cohort, reported with the survey response rate beside the percentage.

Evidence

The charts and formulas above are the working model. A live dashboard will replace the planning figures as measurement begins.
