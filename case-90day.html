Sample 90-Day AI Adoption Plan

A proposed rollout for a trained staff cohort. This is a planning sample I would bring to a new organization and adapt. It is not a report of results already achieved at Beaufort County School District.

Status. Proposed approach Cohort. About 300 trained staff, target Primary outcome. Weekly active use, not attendance

Proposed approach. Targets and illustrative calculations, not achieved results. Figures tagged Target are goals being measured now.

The problem I would start with

A workshop can fill a room and still leave weekly use unchanged. This plan treats attendance as an input. The outcome is whether trained staff use an approved tool on real work at least weekly, whether that work is reviewed, and whether the behavior lasts through 90 days.

The sample uses a school-district cohort because that is the setting I know. The transferable structure (baseline, role-based training, champions, office hours, a department workflow, and 30/60/90 measurement) is what I would carry into a company, with that organization’s tools, policy, and time standards in place of these illustrations.

What I would own in 90 days

  • A baseline of current weekly use, taken before or at the first session, clearly labeled as a survey result.
  • Role-based training, office hours, and short follow-up, not a single event.
  • A champion group with a defined selection rule, responsibilities, and an escalation path.
  • One department workflow, timed, with human review built into the after condition. A candidate in a school setting is staff policy and procedure lookup. That workflow is planned, not built, and is not presented here as a completed implementation.
  • A simple dashboard of weekly use, hours, reach, and champion coverage, with every unverified figure tagged as a target or estimate.

Champion model

The network structure already exists in my current work. The headcount below is a coverage target, not a roster I can publish today. The operating model is proposed:

  • Selection. Practitioners who already use an approved tool and can coach peers, chosen for coverage across sites and functions. Target: 24 by day 90 (8% of 300), stretch 30 (10%).
  • Responsibilities. Model one useful workflow, support colleagues, log barriers, and escalate policy or data questions.
  • Support. Playbooks, the role-based video series, and office hours.
  • Feedback. Short check-ins at 30, 60, and 90 days, plus pulse surveys of the wider cohort.
  • Escalation. Privacy, student-data, or tool-approval issues go to the appropriate district or IT channel. The enablement lead does not assume formal authority over other departments.

A planned workflow, named clearly

If a first timed workflow is approved, the intended task is staff policy and procedure lookup: finding the right policy or procedure in existing documents, with citations and required human review. That project is planned. It has not been built, and no before-and-after times are presented as measurements.

Governance, labeled as proposed

What is already true in my current role is collaboration on adoption methodology in a regulated environment, and evaluation of tools for fit, risk, and responsible use. The following are proposed practices I would put in place with a new organization, not a claim that I have already implemented enterprise governance:

  • A short list of approved tools, with a clear path to request something new.
  • Data-handling rules that keep personal and regulated information out of unapproved systems.
  • Human review before AI output is used in a decision or an external document.
  • A simple quality check (source, accuracy, tone) built into the workflow, not added later.
  • A named escalation path for privacy, legal, or technical issues.
